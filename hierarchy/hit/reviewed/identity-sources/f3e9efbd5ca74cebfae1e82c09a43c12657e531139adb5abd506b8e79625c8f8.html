      简单介绍
      面向事务处理的高性能容错计算机主要应用于国家关键行业，该类计算机要求一方面具有极强的事务处理能力，另一方面具有极高的可用性，可长期提供高速、稳定的信息处理服务，系统的延误和失效可能会造成不可估量的损失。该类系统的可用性精确度量和预估具有重要意义。以面向事务处理的高性能容错计算机作为目标系统，提出一种静态分析与实测相结合的可用性评测方法，通过在系统研制，试运行阶段得到的不充分故障数据和加速测试(故障注入、压力测试)数据，参考经典故障分布，结合不完全覆盖模型，并进行相关度、敏感度分析，建立系统可靠性/可用性模型;进一步，从用户的角度分析可用的标准，用某段时间内系统服务质量的某种对应关系来衡量系统可用性，建立多态系统状态模型，考察可用性和多态模型状态转移概率的关系，选择合适的分析工具进行解析，对系统处于可用状态的概率进行加权作为系统可用性度量指标。

  • 讲授课程
      名称

      1. 《计算机组成原理》：计算机类的专业基础课。课程培养学生“全面系统掌握现代计算机系统的基本组成和内部运行机制，掌握计算机硬件系统的基本分析与设计方法，建立计算机系统的整体概念，具备设计和实现计算机系统，并使用计算机系统高效解决实际问题的能力”为主要目标。通过从整体框架入手，自顶向下，层层细化，通过对计算机系统概述、总线系统、存储系统（包括主存储器、高速缓冲存储器和虚拟存储器）、输入输出系统、中央处理器（包括运算方法和运算器、指令系统、CPU结构及功能），控制单元（包括其功能和组合逻辑以及微程序控制单元的设计思想和实现技术）的深入剖析，为全面培养学生系统认知能力、面向问题求解能力、系统开发与调试能力，以及设计与创新能力奠定良好的基础，并为进一步学习计算机设计与实践、接口技术、嵌入式系统、计算机体系结构、操作系统、计算机网络等后续课程奠定坚实的基础。

      2. 《计算机系统性能评价》: 面向硕士研究生开设的学科专业课。性能评价是计算机网络和计算机系统研究与应用的重要理论基础和支撑技术，本课程介绍了计算机系统、计算机网络等性能评价的基本理论及方法，内容包括性能评价的相关模型及方法。第一部分为系统性能评价的基本理论，包括排队论及排队网络，在此基础上介绍典型计算机系统及网络系统性能评价的基本方法；第二部分为随机Petri网模型方法和分析技术。

      学术学位博士招生:

      基础性研究：领域大模型基于国产异构GPU的运行时性能优化技术

      1）研究内容

      a）研究基于强化学习的内存动态优化方法，智能化实现CPU和国产异构GPU之间数据交换、训练过程中参数重计算，最大化单卡内存使用效率

      b）研究基于缓存动态感知的算子融合方法，结合GPU多层次可编程显存结构，利用低层次显存最大程度减少访存开销，生成高效内核

      c）预测子模型在不同通信模式下的性能开销，以整体通信最优化为指导，自适应选择通信策略

      d）研究基于关键路径最优化执行的图搜索方法、基于强化学习和图搜索结合的方法寻找算子最优分布式并行策略


      专业学位博士招生：

      工程技术攻关研究：国产GPU智算-超算融合技术

      1）研究内容

      a）智慧计算体系结构，多元算力的高效聚合和按需定义，从根本上解决多架构引发的无法兼容、效率不高的问题；

      b）支持多元异构计算、支持新型存储资源池化的高速互连交换网络；

      c）基于软件定义的控制层和基础设施分离、统一资源组织模型、标准化可编程结构；

      d）基于特征感知的自适应应用编排和弹性资源调度；

      e）面向智慧计算的软硬件结合的多层次容错设计技术与验证技术；

      f）分布式异构内存池化技术实现计算架构从以 CPU 为中心到以数据为中心多计算引擎架构的变革。

  • Basic Information
      title

      Zuo Decheng, male, Han ethnicity, born in 1972. He holds a Ph.D., and is a professor and doctoral supervisor. He is the Deputy director of the China Computer Federation's Technical Committee on Fault-Tolerant Computing, the director of the Heilongjiang Provincial Engineering Research Center for High-End Computer Evaluation, and the director of the Fault-Tolerant and Mobile Computing Research Center at Harbin Institute of Technology.

      He received his bachelor's degree from Jilin University in 1994, and his master's and Ph.D. from Harbin Institute of Technology in 1996 and 2000, respectively.

      From 2008 to 2013, he served as the team leader for the overall expert group of the National 863 Program's "High-End Fault-Tolerant Computers" major project. He led the research to develop computer systems and test verification environments that meet the requirements of core information systems for important national economic sectors, such as finance and telecommunications. The project's successful promotion and application broke the monopoly of foreign products.

      From 2012 to 2016, he was a member of the expert group for the Information Technology sector of the Ministry of Science and Technology's 863 Program. Since 2016, he has been a member of the overall expert group for the "High-Performance Computing" key R&D program of the Ministry of Science and Technology.

      In recent years, he has served as a project leader or key technical member on more than 10 major projects, including the National 863 Program, the National Natural Science Foundation of China, the "New Century Excellent Talents Support Program" of the Ministry of Education, the Science and Technology Support Program, ministerial pre-research projects, and provincial science and technology tackling programs.

      He has achieved numerous research results, including five provincial and ministerial-level Science and Technology Progress Awards (two second prizes and three third prizes). He holds 27 national invention patents and 12 software copyrights. He has published more than 100 papers in domestic and international academic journals and conferences, with over 50 of them indexed by SCI/EI/ISTP.

      His main research interests are computer architecture, fault-tolerant computing, and mobile computing.

      Ph.D. Program (Academic Track)

      Academic Research: Runtime Performance Optimization for Domain-Specific Large Models on Domestic Heterogeneous GPUs

      Research Topics:

      a) Investigate reinforcement learning-based dynamic memory optimization methods to intelligently manage data exchange between the CPU and domestic heterogeneous GPUs and parameter re-computation during training, maximizing single-card memory usage efficiency.

      b) Explore dynamically cache-aware operator fusion methods. By leveraging the GPU's multi-level programmable memory structure, use low-level memory to minimize memory access overhead and generate efficient kernels.

      c) Predict the performance overhead of sub-models under different communication patterns and adaptively select communication strategies guided by overall communication optimization.

      d) Research graph-search methods based on critical path optimization and combined reinforcement learning and graph-search methods to find the optimal distributed parallel strategy for operators.

      1. “Computer Organization” is a fundamental course for computer science and engineering majors. The main objective of this course is to equip students with a comprehensive and systematic understanding of the basic components and internal mechanisms of modern computer systems. They will learn fundamental analysis and design methods for computer hardware systems, build a holistic concept of computer systems, and develop the ability to design, implement, and efficiently use computer systems to solve real-world problems.

      Starting with the overall framework and moving from a top-down approach, the course provides an in-depth analysis of: The overview of computer systems, Bus systems, Memory systems (including main memory, cache, and virtual memory), Input/output systems, The central processing unit (CPU), which includes arithmetic methods and the arithmetic logic unit (ALU), instruction sets, and CPU structure and function, Control units (including their functions, combinational logic, and the design and implementation of microprogrammed control units)

      This deep dive lays a solid foundation for cultivating students' systematic cognitive abilities, problem-solving skills, system development and debugging capabilities, as well as their design and innovation skills. It also serves as a strong basis for further studies in subsequent courses like computer design and practice, interface technology, embedded systems, computer architecture, operating systems, and computer networks.

      2. “Computer System Performance Evaluation” is a specialized course for master's students. Performance evaluation is a crucial theoretical foundation and supporting technology for the research and application of computer networks and computer systems.

      The course introduces the fundamental theories and methods of performance evaluation for computer systems and computer networks, covering relevant models and techniques. The course content is divided into two parts:

      Part One covers the basic theory of system performance evaluation, including queuing theory and queuing networks. Building on this foundation, it introduces the fundamental methods for evaluating the performance of typical computer and network systems. Part Two focuses on stochastic Petri net modeling and analysis techniques.
