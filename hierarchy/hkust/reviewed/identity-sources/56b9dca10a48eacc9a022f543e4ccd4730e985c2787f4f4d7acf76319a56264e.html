sehi@ust.hk Google Scholar @sehilyi in/sehilyi

I’m an Assistant Professor at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST)’s Department of Computer Science and Engineering, where I direct the VisAI Lab. Previously, I was an NIH K99/R00 Pathway to Independence Fellow at Harvard Medical School’s Department of Biomedical Informatics and a member of the HIDIVE Lab led by Nils Gehlenborg. I received my PhD in Computer Science and Engineering from Seoul National University under the supervision of Jinwook Seo at the Human-Computer Interaction Lab.

My research explores how people can interact more effectively with artificial intelligence, with a particular focus on biomedical applications. Previously, I led the design and development of the Gosling visualization grammar, which enables the construction of scalable and interactive genomics data visualizations and the leveraging of AI methods (see Nature’s Technology Feature).

My work has been published in top-tier conferences and journals across my fields, including VIS, TVCG, CHI, UIST, Nature Methods, Nature Medicine, npj Digital Medicine, and Bioinformatics. I have received competitive academic awards, including the NIH/NHGRI K99/R00 Pathway to Independence Award, VIS Best Paper Honorable Mention Award, ISMB BioVis Best Abstract Award, ISMB BioVis Runner-Up Abstract Award, and VIS Best Poster Research Award. I have served on the program committee for top visualization conferences, including VIS, EuroVis, and PacificVis.

Media Coverage

Nature (TECHNOLOGY FEATURE)
A graphics toolkit for visualizing genome data
Powerful 'grammar' allows geneticists to display their data in interactive and scalable illustrations.
"Postdoc Sehi L'Yi, who led Gosling's development, says that what differentiates Gosling from other visualization tools is its expressiveness. With most tools, he says, the graphics that can be made and what they will look like are predefined. 'It is really not easy to customize visualizations as a user.' But with Gosling, users can, for instance, specify the colour, dimensions and placement of the symbol used to represent a centromere or genomic interval, then overlay that on an ideogram of a chromosome to highlight a region of interest."
