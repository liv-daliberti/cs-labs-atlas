Hi, I'm Zeke!

I am a PhD student at Virginia Tech, advised by Atul Mantri. I am working on quantum computing, especially in the context of cryptography.

Current Focus

My research centers on the foundations of quantum cryptography, especially primitives which can exist even in worlds where classical cryptography collapses: some examples include pseudorandom unitaries, EFI pairs, and quantum commitments. I am especially intersted in black hole radiation decoding (BHRD) as a computational problem, which both relates tightly to the other primitives mentioned, and offers an escape to a physics paradox! I believe the relationship between quantum cryptography and information scrambling in physics is a promising avenue towards new insights in both.

Undergraduate Work

My undergraduate degrees are in Mathematics and Computer Science from LeTourneau University, with a minor in Physics. I did much to busy myself outside of class, including research, tutoring, various leadership roles, and even rugby. I was able to publish a number theory paper in the Ramanujan Journal during my time as an undergraduate, and learned to two step!
