    How labs, papers and students are decided

    Departments. Every faculty member of the computing departments is covered: Princeton CS; CMU’s seven School of Computer Science departments; MIT EECS; EPFL’s IC school; Berkeley EECS; Stanford CS. Stanford EE lists its faculty who work in computer science. Faculty of any other department are covered when they work in computer science: their papers at the school are in CS venues (indexed by DBLP, the computer-science bibliography; at least 5 papers and a fifth of their output). They appear under their home department, which lists only those faculty.

    Labs. Each faculty PI has one lab: the PI and their advisees. A PI is a faculty member with stated PhD advising or a lab they head. Only labs with at least one lab paper are listed; the count of labs not listed, and the reason for each, are kept with the data.

    Papers. A paper is the lab’s when the PI is on the byline, the paper has a supported school connection, and it falls in the PI’s appointment. The school connection can come from the PI’s affiliation, reviewed paper text, or a stated advisee co-author at the school. A paper whose entire byline has no affiliations can also count when a stated advisee is on it. The appointment is a reviewed faculty period, or a captured department faculty listing from the paper’s own year; a listing after an evidenced move does not extend the appointment. The PI’s identity is anchored by their DBLP person page, their department’s publication list, their verified works, a reviewed OpenAlex author id or ORCID, an advisee co-author, or an OpenAlex author id seen on those.

    Students and lab members. Stated advisees (rosters, theses, alumni pages, FPO announcements) and the people the PI’s own lab pages list: students, and also postdocs, staff and visitors, each with the role the page gives. The headline student count is students only. A student–paper link is the student’s recorded authorship of a lab paper (verified), or a supported name match on that lab paper’s byline (candidate). Candidate matching rejects different full given names, including hyphenated names, and disallows partial-prefix matches under East Asian family names.
