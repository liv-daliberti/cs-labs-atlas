# Full-text figures under two conditions: main body only vs main body + appendix

Status 2026-11-05: the splitter (`ai-writing/fulltext_sections.py`) is built and validated on 500 papers. Nothing else has run: no full re-extraction, no GPU job, `fulltext.sqlite` and every existing output are untouched.

## What the audit of today's "full text" found
- `fulltext.sqlite.paragraphs` holds 226 rows (226 papers). The stored bodies are whitespace-flattened, so `fulltext.py`'s blank-line paragraphs cannot be cut; every consumer (`diversity/common.segments`, `detector_v2`, `dose/dose_score.py`, the embeddings) falls back to `common.passages(body)` (~150-word sentence-bounded passages, 40-400 words, >= 70% printable words, <= 100 per paper). **The unit that needs a `section` tag is the passage, not the paragraph row.**
- Today's body contains, in 130 of 500 pilot papers (26%), text that is not the main body: the reference list (IEEE small-caps `R EFERENCES` is not recognised, so the cut is skipped; 49 of the 130 have no appendix at all) or an appendix that sits before the references or has its own References heading after it. The main body, as the splitter defines it, is contained in today's body in 99.8% of papers (>= 95% of its 10-word shingles).
- Two-column PDFs: pdftotext without `-layout` interleaves columns, so up to one column of references can end up on the main side of the cut (same limit as today's extractor).

## The splitter (done)
`ai-writing/fulltext_sections.py`: `split_file(path)` / `split_text(text)` / `split_xml(raw)` return `main`, `references`, `appendix` and flags (`appendix_found`, `appendix_position` before_refs | after_refs | split, `appendix_rule` appendix | lettered | supplementary | xml_back, `refs_found`, `refs_heuristic`, `intro_found`, `truncated`, `appendix_stub`, `pages`, char and word counts for each part). Main starts where today's body starts (same Introduction rule, plus the small-caps `I NTRODUCTION`) and ends at the first appendix or references heading; main is capped at today's 300,000 characters, appendix at 1,000,000. pdftotext reads up to 400 pages (5-minute limit); `truncated` is set at the cap or at the limits. `passages_tagged(main, appendix, passages)` gives `(section, passage)`: main passages are exactly `common.passages(main)`; appendix passages are cut and capped (100) separately, so the main-only condition is a subset of main+appendix and nothing in it is rescored.
Rules: explicit headings (Appendix / Appendices / APPENDIX A / `A PPENDIX`, Supplementary or Supplemental Material/Information/Data/Figures/Tables, Online/Technical Appendix), lettered top-level headings (`A  Proof of ...`, `A` over a title line, `A.1 ...`) that follow the conclusion or the references and are followed by a `B ...` / `B.1` / `A.2` heading, JATS `<app-group>`/`<app>`, TEI `<div type="annex">`. An explicit "Appendix" heading beats the lettered rule. A heading must be heading-like (<= 12 words, no verb of running prose, no initials, not a running header like `A PREPRINT`), and a "Supplementary Material" heading that only points to files elsewhere (< 100 words, or "available online", "refer to Web version", "for this article can be found online") is a stub, not an appendix.

## Pilot results (500 papers, every boundary checked by reading the text around it)
Samples: seed 20261105 (200, rules tuned here), 20261106, 20261107 (100 each, used for the later rule fixes), 20261108 (100, final rules, held out). Stratified by school x kind (pdf/xml) x year bucket; papers whose stored file is missing on disk were skipped in the draw (see "Risks").
| set | flagged | correct | wrong | missed (seen) | precision | recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (200, tuned) | 64 | 64 | 0 | 1 | 100% | 98% |
| 2 (100, rules before fix 2) | 24 | 24 | 0 | 2 | 100% | 92% |
| 3 (100, rules before fix 3) | 26 | 23 | 3 | 4 | 88% | 85% |
| 4 (100, final rules, held out) | 25 | 23 | 2 (stubs, fixed afterwards) | 1 | 92% | 96% |
Pooled over sets 2-4 (before the fix each one prompted): precision 93%, recall 91% (recall counts only misses I saw; papers with no heading at all are invisible to the outline check, so true recall is somewhat lower). Boundary position: of the correct flags the appendix starts exactly at its heading in ~94%; the rest start a line or two early or late (page number or running header glued on, a reference tail in a two-column PDF, a heading glued to the previous column). Fixes that came out of the held-out sets (appendix letter handling, sentence-like titles, supplementary stubs, figure-panel labels) were re-run on all four sets; no fresh set has seen the final fix.
Failure patterns: (1) appendix without any heading (a math paper whose proofs follow the references with no "Appendix"/"A" heading), (2) supplementary section titles inside the running text ("Supplementary Section S6.1." run-in headings), (3) journal-style lettered sections before the references with symbols in the title ("A. The Harmonic Measure on C1\R>0"), (4) reference list with no heading (3% of PDFs; the heuristic tail trim catches some), (5) Springer/NAR/Frontiers "Supplementary Material ... available online" followed by acknowledgements (stub list grows with each publisher), (6) arXiv "A PREPRINT" running headers.

Prevalence (post-stratified to the 52,023 stored copies dated <= 2025; 498 distinct papers): appendix or supplementary material in **31%** (appendix proper 28%, supplementary-only 3%). By year: 2016-18 21%, 2019-21 26%, 2022-25 39%. By school: Berkeley 43%, Princeton 43%, Stanford 35%, EPFL 29%, CMU 25%, MIT 23%. pdf 31%, xml 22%. By OpenAlex type: preprint 56% (n=103), conference paper 26% (168), journal article 22% (219). Position: after the references 108, before 43. Appendix share of a flagged paper's words: median 23%, mean 27%; over all papers 15% of words are appendix. 1.2% of copies exceed 60 pages (6/498); 4 of the 108 after-refs appendices sit past page 60 and were cut by today's cap. Nothing hit the 400-page cap.
Main vs today's body: shingle containment of today's body in main: median 1.00, 74% of papers >= 0.95 both ways; main contained in today's body: 99.8% (>= 0.95). Identical passage lists: 68% of papers (80% where no appendix was found, 53% with an appendix after the references, 7% with one before). Today's body is longer in 26% of papers (median new/old words 0.78 in those).

## Full run
**Input**: the 59,916 papers of `fulltext.sqlite.texts` (plus the Michigan / UChicago / Georgia Tech and later rows once `fulltext.py` is re-run; the owner's ~78k figure) with their `texts.path` under `STORES`.
**Job**: new `ai-writing/sections/run_split.py` (to be written; the pilot's `one()` is its core) and `run_split.slurm`: array of 4 shards by `rowid % 4`, `--partition=all --account=mltheory --exclude=node302,node014 --cpus-per-task=8 --mem=8G --time=00:59:00`, `sbatch --test-only` first, never submit on "Preempts:". Each shard copies nothing; it reads the stored copies in place, writes node-local `/tmp/sections-<job>.sqlite` and copies it once to `ai-writing/sections/shard-<i>.sqlite`; a merge step (CPU, 1 core, minutes; not measured) builds `ai-writing/sections/sections.sqlite`.
**Measured on the pilot**: 8.7 s user + 0.8 s system for 60 papers in one process (0.16 CPU-s per paper, warm cache), 23 MB resident; 200 papers in 20 s on 6 processes. 78k papers = 3.5 CPU-hours; with cold NFS reads assume 2-3x: about 25-40 minutes per shard at 8 CPUs (20k papers), so 59 minutes is safe and one resubmit covers a failed shard. Disk: ~1.4 GB (main + appendix compressed).
**Missing copies**: in the first, unfiltered 200-paper draw, 7 stored paths (3.5%, all PDFs from EPFL, Stanford and MIT) no longer exist on disk (`fulltext.sqlite` rows came from the homogenization study's earlier extraction). For ~2,700 papers the splitter returns `error: missing`; they keep today's body as `main`, flagged `copy_missing`, and are listed for the owner.
**New tables** (in `sections.sqlite`, not in `fulltext.sqlite`):
- `sections(key PK, school, path, kind, main zlib, appendix zlib, flags JSON, main_words, appendix_words, appendix_found, appendix_position, appendix_rule, truncated, copy_missing, split_version)` (`references` text is not stored, only its length in flags).
- `passages(key, i, section, text_hash, words)`: `common.passages(main)` then `common.passages(appendix)`, `section` in {main, appendix}, `i` continuing (this is the table the owner's `paragraphs.section` becomes; `fulltext.sqlite.paragraphs` stays as is).
- Per-paper `main_words`, `appendix_words` and the flags above feed `ai_scores.sqlite.papers` (new columns `n_passages_main`, `n_passages_appendix`, `ai_share_main`, `ai_share_all`, `body_words_main`, `body_words_appendix`, `appendix_found`, `appendix_position`, `appendix_rule`; the old `ai_share` stays as the legacy column).
**Gate before anything downstream**: audit 100 new papers by reading (the pilot recipe), and compare the counts against the pilot (appendix share 31% +/- 3 points). Re-run the splitter on the pilot keys; flags must reproduce.

## Detector (`detector_v2`, `dose/dose_score.py`): which passages are new
- Passage lists differ for 32% of papers (68% identical). The scored table `detector_v2/passage_scores.jsonl.gz` stores `{key, pidx, words, p_ai, flagged}` with no text hash, so carry-over needs the old lists rebuilt from `fulltext.sqlite` bodies (CPU, minutes; not measured) and matched by passage text; identical passages keep their stored p.
- New appendix passages: 3.15 per paper on average after the printable-word filter (pilot, weighted), i.e. ~245,000 for 78,000 papers (appendices after the references were never scored; those before the references were scored inside the body). About 31% of papers have any.
- New main passages (re-cut because today's body carried references or an appendix): up to ~0.8 million in the worst case (all changed passages), a guess of ~0.4 million.
- GPU: the final model scored 1,409,830 passages on 10 A5000s in 19-29 minutes each (about 350,000 passages per GPU-hour). **0.7 to 3 GPU-hours in total on A5000** (`detector_v2/score_delta.slurm`, `--gres=gpu:a5000:1`, arrays of 4-8, 59 minutes). The delta store is built by `score_delta_prep.py` with a `sections` tag.
- **Calibration gate (new)**: the 1% false-positive threshold (p >= 8.53e-4) was calibrated on pre-ChatGPT body passages. Appendices are proofs, prompts, code and tables; their false-positive rate on 2016-2021 papers must be measured before main+appendix numbers are shown (flagged share of appendix passages of papers dated <= 2021 must be <= 1.5%; if not, report a per-section threshold or drop appendix passages that are mostly symbols). `dose_validate.py` already has the machinery (`--recalibrate`).

## Embeddings (`embeddings/`, `diversity/embed_fulltext.py`)
- Existing full-text vectors are per chunk plus a per-paper mean, bound to `text_hash` of the old body. 73% of papers have a body equal to main (>= 95% containment both ways): keep their vectors as the main condition. For the other 27% (references or an appendix inside today's body) re-embed main.
- Appendix text is embedded as separate chunks for the ~31% of papers that have one; `main+appendix` mean = (n_main * mean_main + n_app * mean_app) / (n_main + n_app) from the stored chunk counts (no re-embedding of main).
- Text volume to embed per model: 1,893 words/paper (re-embedded main, weighted) + 1,313 words/paper (appendix) = 38% of today's 8,516 words/paper. If the 8-model set of 2026-11-04 is already complete on the old bodies, the new runs are the appendix chunks (all 8 models) and the 27% main re-embeds (all 8 models); at today's rates that is about 0.4 of one full-text pass per model. SPECTER2 (`embed_fulltext.py`, `embed_fulltext.slurm`) first, the other models by `embeddings/submit_embed.sh --text`.
- Topics (`embeddings/topics.py`) and novelty (`novelty_run.py --kind fulltext`) are recomputed from the per-condition mean vectors; topics themselves can stay fixed (abstract-based) and only the assignments of full-text vectors are redone.
