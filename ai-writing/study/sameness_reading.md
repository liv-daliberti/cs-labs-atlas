**Answer to the question: the non-text measures do not corroborate the embedding convergence.** Of the four layers, three show no movement toward sameness after 2022 and one (topics) shows concentration that is partly older than ChatGPT. The embedding result therefore stays a statement about abstracts' semantics (which includes topic and wording), not about what labs cite, which benchmarks they name or which methods they name.

1. **Reference overlap (layer 1): nothing detectable.** After removing what popularity of works and list size give, labs of one school share 1.055 -> 1.043 times the overlap of the shuffled null (change -0.012 [-0.092, +0.062]); labs of different schools 0.981 -> 0.947 (-0.034 [-0.089, +0.024]). Cosine coupling agrees. The intervals are wide (about +/-0.07 on a ratio near 1.05) so a convergence as small as the embedding one (about 1% of the scale) could hide here; what is excluded is a large one. On journal papers only (stable reference coverage) overlap beyond the null falls slightly (-0.089 [-0.170, -0.001] within a school, -0.093 [-0.150, -0.026] across schools), i.e. toward diversity. The overlap beyond popularity is tiny in absolute terms (Jaccard excess about 5e-5): labs' reference lists share mostly the works everyone cites.
2. **Citation concentration (layer 2): concentration fell, but the primary sample is confounded by a 2022 break.** The share of references on the top 1% of cited works drops 0.0835 -> 0.0761 (-0.0074 [-0.0098, -0.0048]) and the Gini 0.254 -> 0.231, but the series break in 2022 (figure 2), the year OpenAlex reference lists of repository papers disappear (caveat 1), not in 2023. On journal papers, whose lists stay complete, the top-1% share does not move (-0.0019 [-0.0039, +0.0009]) and the Gini falls -0.0086 [-0.0152, -0.0012] at about the rate of the placebo (-0.0141): a continuing drift toward broader citing, not toward the same papers.
   *Mega-cited papers.* A few works did take off: the fastest riser is cited by 6.8% of labs in 2023-25 against 0.04% before, and 100 such works exist (`tables/ref_mega_works_titles.csv`; most are outside the store, so only their OpenAlex ids are known: ids W43... and W67... are works indexed after 2022 and the profile fits LLM-era papers, but this was not verified by title). Removing them lowers the post-period top-1% share further (-0.0138 instead of -0.0074) and the canonical share (cited by >= 5% of labs: -0.0057 instead of -0.0035): they add about 0.6 points of top-1% share to 2023-25 (and 0.2 points of canonical share), more than offset by dispersal elsewhere. Removing the 100 most cited works overall changes little. So the rise of a few mega-cited papers is real and visible, and it is not broad concentration.
3. **Benchmarks, datasets and methods (layer 3): dispersal and a wider vocabulary, not clustering.** In abstracts the HHI of benchmark+dataset names falls 0.0723 -> 0.0507 (-0.0215 [-0.0354, -0.0089]; the placebo is not distinguishable from zero) and the entropy rises +0.51 bits; for methods HHI falls -0.0113 [-0.0137, -0.0098] and entropy rises +0.50 bits (the placebo points the same way, smaller). Among labs that name any benchmark/dataset, the share using a top-5 entry falls 0.606 -> 0.465. The share of all labs naming a method or benchmark at all rises (+11 points for methods), the footprint of the LLM era in abstracts, and it is the reason the unconditional "share of labs using a top-5 entry" rises for methods (+0.057): it is not clustering. The full-text main-body sample agrees on sign (HHI -0.0257 and -0.0098) but covers different shares of papers in different years. Hand check: 29 of 30 extractions correct.
4. **Topics (layer 4): the one place that moves toward sameness, and it is the LLM shift.** Concentration of OpenAlex primary topics rises: HHI 0.0049 -> 0.0072 (+0.0023 [+0.0019, +0.0028], above the placebo +0.0014) and the effective number of topics falls 409 -> 333; at subfield level the same sign (+0.0153) with a placebo of similar size (+0.0129). This is consistent with LLM topics absorbing papers (main RESULTS: +15.65 points of papers on LLM topics). The cross-lab JSD of subfield mixes falls (-0.042 [-0.056, -0.027] within a school) but did so before 2023 too (placebo -0.030 [-0.044, -0.015]); with a fixed set of large labs the post change (-0.031) is smaller than the placebo (-0.040): a long-running drift, not a post-2022 break. The pooled-period JSD of the main study (-0.0068 [-0.0135, +0.0008]) is the more conservative number; the yearly one is sensitive to which labs have >= 10 papers in a year.

**Comparison with the embeddings.** Embeddings: across labs -0.0113 and across schools -0.0104 in distance (about 1% of the scale), with a placebo of the opposite sign (+0.006): a clear post-2022 break toward sameness. Non-text: references no change (journal: slightly more diverse); citation concentration down (confounded by the 2022 coverage break; journal-only: flat or slow drift); benchmarks and methods more dispersed; topics more concentrated but on the LLM shift and with a pre-existing drift. **Diverge or show nothing, not converge**, except coarse topic concentration. What would still support "research sameness": nothing in these four layers on its own; the defensible statement is that abstracts of different labs became semantically a little closer while their reference lists, cited works and named benchmarks and methods did not.
